About Ballot Facts

A free, nonpartisan look at what's on your ballot: who's running, who's paying for them, and what's been fact-checked. Built for voters, not campaigns.

What we collect: nothing

  • Your address is sent to the US Census Bureau (to find your districts) and Google's Civic Information API (to find polling places), once, when you press the button. We don't store it, log it, or put it in the page address.
  • No cookies, no accounts, no analytics, no ads. The site loads nothing from any other company: no fonts, scripts or trackers.
  • No JavaScript. Every page is plain HTML and CSS.

Don't take our word for it

  • All of the code is public at {{ config.REPO | replace("https://", "") }}, under the AGPL licence.
  • The footer of every page shows the exact version of the code this site is running. Click it to see that version.
  • Your browser enforces our rule against outside content: every page sends a Content-Security-Policy header that blocks scripts and loading anything from other sites. You can see it in your browser's developer tools under Network.

Who runs this

One person: DieLaughing, as part of InPints, a small group of independent hobby projects. Not a campaign, party, PAC or advocacy group, and not affiliated with any of the data sources below.

Who pays for this

One person, out of pocket. It runs on a small rented server alongside a few hobby projects. Ballot Facts takes no donations, no sponsorships, no advertising and no money from any campaign, party, PAC or advocacy group. If that ever changes, every dollar will be listed on this page.

Where the data comes from

  • Districts: US Census Bureau geocoder, using the 2026 district maps.
  • Federal candidates and money: Federal Election Commission (OpenFEC API). Updated about daily.
  • Polling places and official links: the Voting Information Project, through Google's Civic Information API.
  • Fact checks: Google Fact Check Tools, which collects ClaimReview data published by fact-checkers.
  • State legislators: Open States.
  • Massachusetts state and county races: official 2026 primary results from the Secretary of the Commonwealth's ElectionStats; ballot questions from the state's Information for Voters booklet. The script that collects them is in the repo, so anyone can rerun it and compare.
  • Massachusetts campaign money: the state's Office of Campaign and Political Finance (OCPF): money raised and spent, where it came from, and independent expenditure PAC spending for or against each candidate.
  • Campaign websites: federal candidates' come from their own FEC registration. Others are added only after a person checks that the site names the candidate and the office; the evidence is shown on the candidate's page and kept in websites.json.

We don't call races, endorse candidates, or rate claims. Where a source has gaps, we say so.

How fresh is it?

  • Federal money, outside spending and fact checks are fetched live and kept for a few hours.
  • Massachusetts races and ballot questions come from a snapshot of the official primary results; each page shows when it was collected.
